**Vespertine 1.2.4.** The free, open-source Mac player for surround and hi-res music collections.

If you're new here: Vespertine plays your stereo and surround music from one library (FLAC, DSD, SACD and DVD-Video images, DTS CDs, Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, Atmos from Dolby Digital Plus), shows every step between the file and your DAC, measures and corrects your room with a USB microphone, and tells you whether your "hi-res" files really are. No account, no telemetry, no subscription.

### New

- **Sonos (beta).** Your Sonos rooms show up as outputs, one row per room or per group of rooms playing together. Surrounds, a Sub and the second speaker of a stereo pair belong to their room and don't get rows of their own. Turn it on in Settings › Playback › Network Players (Sonos and UPnP, beta). It stays off until you do: Vespertine doesn't look for players or open a port for them while it's off.
- **The best Sonos takes, and nothing hidden.** Sonos plays two-channel music up to 24-bit / 48 kHz from a computer (16-bit on S1 speakers). A FLAC or Apple Lossless file within that goes to Sonos as the file itself. Vespertine converts anything else to FLAC: a hi-res song at 48 or 44.1 kHz (whichever divides its own rate), DSD as PCM, surround folded to stereo. The signal path and the badge say what changed and what was sent. Sonos takes no surround and no Dolby Atmos from a computer, only from a TV or a streaming service in its own app, and the room's notes in Vespertine say so.
- **Sonos volume and room details.** A slider sets the room's own volume, as in the Sonos app, and leaves the samples alone. The room's details show its speakers and, for a home theater room, how its surrounds play music (Ambient or Full, which you set in the Sonos app).
- **What each output will play.** With a song loaded, every row in the output list says what that output would get, for example "Will play 24-bit / 48 kHz FLAC, stereo (source 24/96)". The line turns copper when the samples that arrive differ from the file's. It covers DACs, HDMI, Bluetooth, AirPlay, Sonos and other network players. A player that can take neither the file nor FLAC says why.

### Fixed

- A surround file whose channels have no speaker names (a 6-channel WAV with no channel mask, as some tools write) played as silence when folded to stereo. Vespertine now reads it in WAV order, so a 6-channel file folds as 5.1.
- Network players: a Stop or another song you choose now wins over what the player last reported about the song before it. That song no longer shows as started, jumps back to an older position or reports an error after you've moved on. A pause or resume the player refused stays quiet once you've given a newer command.
- Network players: pause, seek, skip and stop go through at once while Vespertine converts or downloads the next song, and a skip or stop gives that work up.

Vespertine's Sonos support has been tested against a simulated Sonos and Sonos's documentation, not yet on a real Sonos system. Reports are welcome.

### Install

Download **Vespertine-1.2.4.dmg**, or `brew install --cask szeremeta1/tap/vespertine`. macOS 14.4 or later, Apple silicon and Intel. Signed with a Developer ID, notarized and stapled.

Already installed? Vespertine updates itself, or run `brew upgrade --cask vespertine`.
